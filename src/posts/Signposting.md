<span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;">One crucial element of any successful large site is navigation. When a site is offering a large number of different services or products, you </span>can't<span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;"> hope to present them all to the user on arrival. Without successful navigation techniques users will quickly get lost in the site and be unable to find what they are looking for.</span>

<span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">Signpost pages can play a pivotal role in aiding a user's navigation through the often numerous and complex choices available to them.</span>

<span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">On many sites, signpost pages take two main forms: landing pages and section pages.</span>

## Landing Pages

A landing page is the page on which a user will enter your site. Often, the homepage is the obvious landing page, but it is increasingly likely that your users will have searched before getting to your site, and so will first arrive  somewhere other than your homepage.

<span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">This Landing Page will be one of the following page types:</span>

1.  <span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;">“The Page” - the right page for the information that they are looking for.</span>
2.  <span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">A section page, which is not “The Page”, but which will lead them directly to “The Page”.</span>
3.  <span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">The wrong place on the site.</span>
<span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">While your aim with any site is to optimise the search to the point where the user always lands on the exact page they need, you cannot cater for all users and all searches. People can search very generically, with terms such as “council tax”, where several distinct pages will exist within that subject.</span>

<span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">And when users land on your site somewhere before the page they need, they will need a signpost to lead them to where they need to be.</span>

## Section pages

Unless the user lands on the right page first time, they might well journey through several pages before they reach their goal. At each stage, the user is simply looking to answer the basic questions:

1.  <span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;">Is this “The Page”? Does it contain the information I need?</span>
2.  <span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;">If this </span>isn't<span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;"> “The Page”, then how do I get to “The Page”?</span>
<span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">This is where section pages are useful.</span>

## 1\. Simple and Different

Section pages are all about answering this second question efficiently. Section pages never have the information the user is looking for, and they should quickly communicate that fact to the user. The best way to do that is to be brief, and to be different.

<span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;">Be Simple: </span>Don't<span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;"> fill the signpost page with content. When users are trying to find specific information, they don’t need paragraphs of text telling them things they </span>didn't<span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;"> want to know. It just creates confusion.</span>

<span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">Be Different: Navigational pages should look different to content pages, so users can quickly differentiate between pages with information and pages that signpost.</span>

<span style="font-size: 1.285714286rem; line-height: 1.6;">2\. Focus on the task in hand</span>

Your users might have many and varied tasks in mind when deciding to visit your site, but they will only have one when on a signpost page: How do I get to The Page with the information I need?

<span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">Section pages need to focus on this task, and deliver the right page, quickly and efficiently to the user.</span>

<span style="font-size: 1.285714286rem; line-height: 1.6;">3\. Provide the right “scent”</span>

There are many usability features you can implement to aid the user in finding their way from the section page, to the real information, and you should certainly use them. But the key to solving your user’s problem, lies in what the page says.

<span style="line-height: 1.714285714; font-size: 1rem;">[Information “scent](http://www.nngroup.com/articles/wrong-information-scent-costs-sales/)” is the term used to describe how people evaluate navigation options. When a user is working out where to go next, they will choose the path that gives off the strongest “scent” of the information they are after.</span>

<span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;">When building section pages, the names given to the options play a critical role in defining this scent. Too vague, and no one will truly understand what lies below the link. Too specific, and many people will disregard an option, thinking it </span>doesn't<span style="font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.714285714;"> contain what they want to know when, in fact, it does.</span>
